How Is Markup Calculated?

Markup is the amount added to the cost of a product to determine its selling price.

Markup Profit = Cost × Markup Percentage ÷ 100

Selling Price = Cost + Markup Profit

For example, if a product costs 500 and you apply a 30% markup:

500 × 30 ÷ 100 = 150

Selling price:

500 + 150 = 650

Markup vs Profit Margin

Markup and profit margin are related but they measure profit from different reference points.

Markup compares profit with the original cost.

Profit margin compares profit with the selling price.

Markup % = Profit ÷ Cost × 100

Profit Margin % = Profit ÷ Selling Price × 100

For example, a product that costs 100 and sells for 150 has a profit of 50.

Markup: 50%

Profit margin: 33.33%

This difference is important when comparing pricing strategies.

What Is a 20% Markup?

A 20% markup means adding an amount equal to 20% of the original cost.

For a cost of 1,000:

1,000 × 20 ÷ 100 = 200

Selling price:

1,000 + 200 = 1,200

What Is a 30% Markup?

A 30% markup on a cost of 500 is:

500 × 30 ÷ 100 = 150

The selling price becomes:

500 + 150 = 650

What Is a 50% Markup?

A 50% markup adds half of the original cost.

If a product costs 800:

800 × 50 ÷ 100 = 400

Selling price:

800 + 400 = 1,200

How to Calculate Markup From Cost and Selling Price

If you already know the cost and selling price, you can calculate markup using:

Markup % = (Selling Price − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100

Example:

Cost = 200
Selling Price = 260

Profit = 60

60 ÷ 200 × 100 = 30% markup

Frequently Asked Questions

How is markup calculated?

Multiply cost by the markup percentage and divide by 100.

How do I calculate selling price?

Add the markup profit to the original cost.

Is markup the same as profit margin?

No. Markup uses cost as the reference value, while profit margin uses selling price.

What is a 30% markup on 500?

The markup profit is 150 and the selling price is 650.

What markup doubles the cost?

A 100% markup doubles the original cost. For example, a cost of 100 becomes a selling price of 200.

Can I use this calculator with any currency?

Yes. The calculation uses numerical values only, so the same currency should be used consistently for cost, profit and selling price.
